# Corrected Strategy: The Honest Conversation with Sharmila Krishnan

> **For:** SAJB Committee / Coordinator  
> **Date:** 15 August 2026  
> **Subject:** Replacing the "doorstep denial" framing with a real human ask

---

## 1. What We Got Wrong

The earlier briefing assumed **defeat before we knocked.** It treated CFLI guidelines as an iron wall and told SAJB to ask for something "safe" instead of what we actually need.

That was a mistake.

**The truth:** We are not submitting a random online form into a black box. We are reaching out to **Sharmila Krishnan**, a human officer at the High Commission of Canada, to start a **relationship**.

The goal of the first email is **not** to flawlessly execute a pre-approved project concept. The goal is to:
- Introduce SAJB as a serious, frontline organisation.
- Be **bluntly honest** about what we need and what we know about CFLI rules.
- Invite **her** to tell us where the line is — and whether there is any room on the other side of it.

---

## 2. What We Actually Need (No Apologies)

Our halfway home is failing because **we do not control the building.**

| Symptom | Root Cause |
|---------|------------|
| Leaking pipes, soaring water bills | We rent. We cannot force the landlord to fix infrastructure. |
| Constant disrepair | We rent. We cannot renovate structurally. |
| Forced co-housing of men and women | We do not have enough space, and we cannot expand or reconfigure the layout. |
| Threat of relocation / rent hikes | We have **zero tenure security.** |
| Volunteer burnout | We are spending 70% of our energy managing a building we do not own instead of serving people. |

**The fix is not a workshop. The fix is a building we control.**

We want one of three outcomes:
1. **A new property** — purchased or allocated to SAJB so we can house people safely and permanently.
2. **A long-term allocation** — government or institutional premises transferred to SAJB for shelter use.
3. **Capital funding for property purchase** — a grant or concessionary loan that lets us buy a suitable building and escape the rental trap.

We know this is a big ask. We are not hiding from that.

---

## 3. What We Know (And What We Don't)

We are aware of the following:

- CFLI typically funds **projects**, not **property**.
- CFLI caps are modest (average CAD $20k–$40k) — not enough to buy a building outright.
- CFLI is designed for **advocacy, capacity-building, and pilot initiatives**, not capital acquisition.
- GAC has **other funding streams** — some larger, some bilateral, some multiyear — but we do not know which ones are open to Malaysian NGOs or how to access them.

**What we don't know:**
- Whether Sharmila's portfolio includes anything **beyond** CFLI.
- Whether Canada has a **rapid response** or **pro-democracy / human rights facilities** fund that *can* support shelter infrastructure.
- Whether the High Commission has ever **broked a partnership** between a local NGO and a Canadian organisation that *does* have capital funding.
- Whether there is a **Canadian NGO or diaspora group** already working on housing rights in Malaysia that SAJB could partner with.
- Whether the High Commission has **discretionary flexibility** to stretch CFLI guidelines in exceptional cases.

We are coming to her **precisely because** we don't know.

## 4. The Reframed Introductory Email

**To:** Sharmila Krishnan (and/or the CFLI desk at the High Commission)  
**From:** [Your Name], Committee Member, Shechinah Association JB  
**Subject:** Seeking Your Guidance — A Structural Crisis in Johor Bahru's Transitional Housing

---

Dear Ms. Krishnan,

I hope this message finds you well. My name is **[Your Name]**, and I serve on the committee of **Shechinah Association JB (SAJB)** — a volunteer-run non-profit in Johor Bahru that provides transitional shelter, food aid, and rehabilitation support to vulnerable and marginalised individuals.

I am writing to you not with a polished proposal, but with an honest question — and I hope you will forgive the directness.

**Our situation:** SAJB operates a halfway home for people transitioning out of incarceration, addiction recovery, and homelessness. Our work is entirely volunteer-driven, and we have built trusted relationships with welfare agencies, religious institutions, and local communities across southern Johor.

But we are trapped by our own building. We rent our premises. The infrastructure is deteriorating — chronic plumbing leaks have sent our water bills beyond what we can sustain, the physical space is too small, and we are currently forced to house men and women together in conditions that compromise safety and dignity. We have no tenure security; our rent has risen twice in two years, and our landlord has indicated the property may be sold.

We cannot renovate a building we do not own. We cannot expand rooms we do not control. And we cannot keep asking our volunteers to plug gaps that require structural solutions.

**What we need is a building we control.** That may mean:
- Capital support to purchase a modest property suitable for shelter use;
- An allocation or concession of unused government or institutional space; or
- A funding mechanism that allows a small organisation like ours to secure permanent premises.

**I am fully aware** that the Canada Fund for Local Initiatives is project-focused, not a capital fund, and that typical CFLI contributions are not sized for real estate. I am not writing to you with the expectation that CFLI will buy us a house.

But I am writing because **you are the person at the High Commission who understands how Canadian assistance reaches Malaysian civil society.** And because I would rather ask you directly than guess wrongly from a website.

My question is simply this: **Does Canada — through CFLI or any other GAC mechanism — engage with organisations facing structural barriers like ours?** And if not through direct funding, might the High Commission be able to advise us on:
- other Canadian or multilateral funding windows open to Malaysian NGOs;
- Canadian civil society partners already working on housing or shelter infrastructure in Malaysia; or
- any pathway — even unconventional — that could help SAJB secure a permanent, safe home for the people we serve?

I have no interest in wasting your time with an application that is destined for the wrong inbox. I would genuinely value **ten minutes of your guidance** — by email, phone, or in person — to understand whether there is any room for SAJB in Canada's Malaysia portfolio, and if so, how we should position ourselves.

I have attached a brief organisational profile and a one-page summary of our current shelter crisis for context. We are grateful for any direction you can offer.

Warm regards,

**[Your Full Name]**

## 5. Why This Approach Is Better

| Old Approach (Defeatist) | New Approach (Honest + Open) |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| "CFLI won't fund buildings, so let's ask for a workshop instead." | "We know CFLI usually doesn't fund buildings. We're asking **you** if there's any exception, workaround, or parallel stream." |
| Hides the real need behind a "fundable" proxy. | Names the real need upfront and treats the officer as a thinking partner, not a gatekeeper. |
| Assumes the officer has zero discretion. | Acknowledges that officers have networks, knowledge of other programmes, and the ability to redirect promising organisations. |
| Risks creating a project SAJB doesn't really want. | Keeps SAJB's integrity intact — we are asking for what we need, not performing for a grant. |
| If rejected, SAJB has nothing. | If rejected for the building, the door is still open for CFLI advocacy funding — and SAJB has established a human relationship. |

## 6. What Happens Next (Two-Way Possibilities)

**Scenario A: Sharmila says "No, Canada has no capital mechanism for this."**
- We thank her sincerely.
- We ask: *"Given what you now know about SAJB, is there a CFLI project we could design that would strengthen our case with other funders?"*
- We pivot to the "Dignity in Transition" advocacy concept — but now with an established contact who already knows our name.

**Scenario B: Sharmila says "CFLI can't, but let me check if..."**
- She may know of a Canadian church group, diaspora association, or INGO with a housing fund.
- She may flag a **rapid response** or **human rights defenders** stream we have never heard of.
- She may say: *"CFLI can't buy property, but if you had a credible 18-month advocacy campaign to get the state to allocate premises, CFLI could fund the campaign."*
- Any of these is a win.

**Scenario C: Sharmila does not reply.**
- We follow up once after two weeks.
- If still no reply, we send the same email (slightly shortened) to `klmpr@international.gc.ca` with a polite note that we are seeking guidance on Canadian funding for shelter infrastructure.
- We do not burn the relationship by spamming.

## 8. The Bottom Line

We are not going to Sharmila with our hat in our hands, pretending we want something else.

We are going to her as a **credible frontline organisation** that has done its homework, knows the rules, and is **brave enough to ask for what we actually need** while respecting her time and expertise.

If the answer is no, we have lost nothing — and we have gained a contact.

If the answer is "let me think..." we have opened a door no website could have opened.

**Send the email.**
