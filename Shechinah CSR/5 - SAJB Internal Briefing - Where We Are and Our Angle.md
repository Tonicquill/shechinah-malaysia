## 1. The Opportunity

The **High Commission of Canada in Kuala Lumpur** runs a small grants programme called the **Canada Fund for Local Initiatives (CFLI)**.

From the **CFLI 2026 Info Session Slides** we have on file (High Commission of Canada Kuala Lumpur), the programme's stated purpose is:

> *"The CFLI is a programme designed to support small-scale, high-impact projects in developing countries, which align with Global Affairs Canada's thematic priority areas for engagement. The programme is directed at projects conceived and designed predominantly by local partners, and is intended to yield tangible results."*

Funding parameters from the same document:

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Average contribution | **CAD $20,000 to $40,000** |
| Maximum per project | **CAD $100,000** |
| Project duration | Single-year (12 months) or two-year (24 months) |
| Application deadline (2026) | 13 April 2026 |
| Project completion (single-year) | 26 February 2027 |
| Eligible applicants | Locally registered NGOs, civil society organisations, community groups |

The next round — **CFLI 2027** — has **not opened yet**. Based on the 2026 cycle, it will likely open around **January–March 2027**.

**Our contact:** We are reaching out to **Sharmila Krishnan** at the Canada High Commission for an **initial conversation** — but **only if the committee agrees on what we ask for**.

## 2. What Canada Actually Cares About (Direct Quotes)

From the **CFLI 2026 Info Session Slides** we hold, Canada's four thematic priority areas for engagement in Malaysia were:

1. **Inclusive governance, democracy, and human rights**
2. **Peace and security** — including Women, Peace and Security; countering cybercrime and online scam centres; youth and peacebuilding
3. **Inclusive growth and women's economic security** — including financial inclusion, decent work, and the care economy
4. **Environment and climate action** — including just transition, nature-based solutions, and climate adaptation

Crucially, the slides state:

> *"Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls (GE) is a cross-cutting theme and must be integrated into all CFLI projects. Projects that do not meaningfully address gender equality will not be funded."*

And further:

> *"Projects should demonstrate how they will advance the human rights of women and girls, including by addressing barriers to their full participation in political, economic, and social life."*

**This is not abstract for us.** Our halfway home is **forced to house men and women together** because we lack space and cannot expand a rented building. This directly violates the dignity, safety, and human rights of the women in our care. It is a **gender equality failure happening in real time** — and SAJB sees it every day.

## 3. The Real Problem (No Sugar-Coating)

Our halfway home is failing because **we do not control the building.**

- **Leaking pipes** = hemorrhaging water bills we cannot afford.
- **Rent hikes / relocation threat** = zero tenure security.
- **Not enough space** = we are **forced to house men and women together**, which is undignified and unsafe.
- **Building falling apart** = we cannot renovate what we do not own.

Our volunteers are spending their energy managing a building crisis instead of serving people. **The root cause is that we rent.**

**What we actually need:**
1. A **new property** we can purchase or secure permanently.
2. A **long-term allocation** of government or institutional premises.
3. **Capital funding** that lets us buy a building and escape the rental trap permanently.

**But here's the direct link to Canada's priorities:** The women in our halfway home cannot sleep safely because there is no separate space. There are no locks. There is no privacy. This is a **human rights and gender equality issue** that Canada explicitly says it cares about. Our building crisis is not just a leaky roof — it is a daily dignity violation that Canada claims it wants to address.

## 4. Two Options for the Committee

We present **two distinct angles** to the committee. Both are honest. Both are valid. The committee should choose which one to lead with — or whether to ask Sharmila about both.

---

### Option A: The Honest Ask (Property / Capital)

**The pitch:** We tell Sharmila exactly what we need — a building — and ask if any Canadian mechanism can help.

I have researched CFLI. Here's what I know:

- CFLI is **project-focused**, not a capital fund.
- Typical contributions are **too small to buy a building** outright.
- CFLI generally funds advocacy, training, and pilots — **not bricks and mortar**.

**BUT** — we are not submitting into a faceless online portal. We are approaching **one human officer**, Sharmila Krishnan, who understands how Canadian assistance reaches Malaysian civil society. She may know:
- Whether **other GAC funding streams** exist that *can* support shelter infrastructure.
- Whether Canada has **rapid response or human rights facilities** funds we have never heard of.
- Whether there is a **Canadian NGO or diaspora group** already working on housing in Malaysia that SAJB could partner with.
- Whether there is any **discretionary flexibility** in exceptional cases.

**Our proposed angle:**

> *"We know CFLI usually doesn't fund property. We are not writing to waste anyone's time. We are writing because we are a credible frontline organisation with a genuine structural crisis, and we want to know — from the person who actually knows the system — whether there is any Canadian pathway that could help us secure a permanent home for the people we serve. And if not, we would value guidance on where else to look."*

This is **not** asking CFLI to buy us a house and pretending we don't know the rules. This is **being honest about the rules and asking whether there is any room on the other side of them.**

**What we are NOT doing with Option A:**
- Pretending we only want a workshop when what we need is a building.
- Submitting a proxy project that SAJB doesn't actually want.
- Acting like we don't know CFLI is project-focused. We do know. That's why we're asking a human.

---

### Option B: "Dignity in Transition" — Change the System, Change Malaysia

**The pitch:** We accept that CFLI will not buy us a building. Instead, we use Canadian funding to **expose and fix the systemic failure** that forces shelters like ours to house men and women together.

From the **CFLI 2026 slides**:

> *"Projects should demonstrate how they will advance the human rights of women and girls, including by addressing barriers to their full participation in political, economic, and social life."*

SAJB is uniquely placed to do this. We are a frontline shelter. We live the problem. We can document it, name it, and push for change.

**What the Canada money would pay for:**
1. **Survey and document** shelter conditions across 5–8 community-run facilities in JB (including ours) — specifically documenting **gender mixing, lack of privacy, and GBV risks**.
2. **Develop a "Safe Shelter Standards" guide** — practical, low-cost rules that any small shelter can follow to protect women.
3. **Run advocacy workshops** with local councillors, state assembly members, and religious leaders to get them to adopt the standards.
4. **Pilot small fixes at SAJB** — privacy partitions, staggered use hours, female chaperone protocols. **Low-cost, immediate dignity measures.**
5. **Public awareness** — a short video or campaign so the community understands that women in shelters deserve safety.

**Why this is powerful:**
- It directly addresses the **gender equality** mandate that the CFLI slides call **mandatory**.
- It turns our crisis into **evidence**.
- It positions SAJB as a **policy actor**, not just a struggling charity.
- It builds **networks** with officials who can eventually help us with premises, allocations, or referrals.
- It comes with **CAD $35,000–$45,000** for activities.

**What SAJB does NOT get from Option B:**
- A new building.
- Our rent paid.
- Our pipes fixed.
- Our water bill covered.

Those things still need to come from somewhere else. But Option B gives us **credibility, evidence, and allies** that make getting a building easier later.
