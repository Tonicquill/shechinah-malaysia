# SAJB Halfway Home Crisis & CFLI Funding Reality Check

> **Date:** 15 August 2026  
> **Author:** Claude / SAJB Committee  
> **Purpose:** Brutally honest mapping of SAJB's real operational crisis against what CFLI can and cannot fund, plus reframed project concepts that are actually eligible.

---

## 1. The Raw Situation (Operational Reality)

SAJB operates a **halfway home / transitional shelter** for vulnerable populations in Johor Bahru. The facility is currently in crisis:

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Constant physical disrepair** | Structural deterioration, unsafe conditions, rising maintenance burden on volunteers |
| **Rent hikes / relocation pressure** | Financial instability; threat of displacement; no tenure security |
| **Pipeline leakages** | Hemorrhaging water bills; diverted funds from food/medicine/care; unsanitary conditions |
| **Severe space constraints** | **Men and women forced to house together** — dignity violation, privacy violation, elevated GBV risk |
| **Volunteer capacity stretched** | 50 volunteers trying to plug operational gaps that need capital investment and professional facilities management |

**In plain terms:** SAJB is holding the line for a population that government services are failing, but the physical infrastructure is collapsing under the load.

---

## 2. The Hard Truth: What CFLI CANNOT Fund

CFLI is a **foreign policy tool**, not an operational charity funder. Based on CFLI guidelines (2026 cycle and historical), the following are **typically ineligible**:

| Ineligible Need | Why CFLI Says No |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Building repair / renovation | Capital expenditure; not within mandate |
| Rent or mortgage payments | Operational subsidy; no sustainable development outcome |
| Utility bills (water, electricity) | Recurrent costs; not a project |
| Purchase of land or buildings | Capital acquisition; excluded by policy |
| Straightforward shelter operations | Seen as charitable relief, not empowerment or rights-based change |
| General food / clothing distribution | Humanitarian relief is covered by other GAC streams (not CFLI) |

**If SAJB submits a CFLI proposal saying "we need money to fix our pipes and separate the men and women," it will be rejected.**

---

## 3. The Opportunity: What CFLI CAN Fund (Reframing the Crisis)

CFLI **can** fund the **systemic, rights-based response** to this crisis. The trick is to move from "SAJB needs money for its building" to **"Johor Bahru's transitional housing system fails women, and SAJB is the local actor best placed to change that."**

Below are **three reframed project concepts** that honestly emerge from SAJB's operational reality but meet CFLI eligibility criteria.

---

### Concept A: Gender-Responsive Transitional Housing Advocacy
**Thematic pillar:** Inclusive governance, democracy & human rights  
**CFLI sub-theme:** Human rights; gender equality; access to justice

**Problem statement (fundable version):**
Johor Bahru lacks gender-segregated, dignified transitional housing for recently incarcerated persons, recovering addicts, and homeless individuals. Current shelters — including SAJB's — are forced by capacity constraints to house men and women together, violating international human rights standards and exposing women to heightened risks of gender-based violence, re-traumatisation, and barriers to rehabilitation. There is **no local policy or standard** governing gender-responsive design in community-run transitional shelters.

**What CFLI would fund:**
- Community-led participatory assessment of shelter conditions across 5–8 community shelters in Johor Bahru (including SAJB's)
- Development of a **"Gender-Responsive Transitional Shelter Standards"** guideline document (adapted from UN OHCHR / UN Women standards)
- Advocacy workshops with local councillors, State Assembly representatives, and religious leaders to adopt standards
- Pilot of a **"Safe Space Protocol"** at SAJB (room dividers, staggered hours, female chaperone system — **low-cost physical adaptations**, not construction)
- Media / visibility campaign: "Dignity in Transition" — public awareness on the right to safe, gender-segregated shelter

**What CFLI would NOT fund:**
- Building a new wing
- Paying SAJB's rent
- Major plumbing works

**Budget estimate:** CAD $35,000 – $45,000  
**Duration:** 12 months

---

### Concept B: Women-Led Water Resilience & Climate Adaptation in Low-Income Shelters
**Thematic pillar:** Environment & climate action  
**CFLI sub-theme:** Climate adaptation; just transition; inclusive green solutions

**Problem statement (fundable version):**
Unregulated water infrastructure in older low-income and community-run buildings across Johor Bahru leads to chronic leakage, inflating utility costs and diverting scarce resources away from care. Women — as primary caregivers and shelter managers — bear the disproportionate burden of managing these failures. There is no community-level knowledge base on low-cost water auditing, rainwater harvesting, or climate-adaptive maintenance for informal shelters.

**What CFLI would fund:**
- Training of **women shelter managers** (including SAJB volunteers) as community water auditors
- Development of a **simple toolkit**: "Low-Cost Water Resilience for Community Shelters in Tropical climates"
- Installation of **rainwater harvesting demonstration units** at 2–3 shelters (including SAJB) — **pilot-scale, not full building retrofit**
- Community forums with local water authorities (SAJ) to report systemic leakage in rental properties housing vulnerable groups
- Documentary / short video series featuring women shelter managers as climate adaptation actors

**What CFLI would NOT fund:**
- Replacing SAJB's entire plumbing system
- Paying SAJB's past water bills
- Commercial plumbing contracts

**Budget estimate:** CAD $30,000 – $40,000  
**Duration:** 12 months

---

### Concept C: Mapping & Securing Dignified Housing Rights for the Recently Incarcerated
**Thematic pillar:** Peace & security / Inclusive governance  
**CFLI sub-theme:** Access to justice; social cohesion; reintegration

**Problem statement (fundable version):**
Recently incarcerated individuals returning to Johor Bahru face a **housing rights gap**: no government transitional housing, discriminatory rental practices, and community shelters that are physically unsafe and overcrowded. This gap increases recidivism risk and destabilises families. There is no civil-society-led data on this population's housing barriers, and no platform for them to advocate for their rights.

**What CFLI would fund:**
- Participatory research: **"Housing Barriers of the Recently Incarcerated in Johor Bahru"** — led by peer researchers (formerly incarcerated persons trained by SAJB)
- Publication of a community report with anonymised findings and recommendations
- Formation of a **"Dignified Reintegration Coalition"** — SAJB + legal aid clinic + religious leaders + employers
- Advocacy meeting with Johor state housing and welfare departments
- Small advocacy grants to coalition members for targeted follow-on activities

**What CFLI would NOT fund:**
- Rent deposits for individuals
- Building renovation
- Operational shelter costs

**Budget estimate:** CAD $35,000 – $50,000  
**Duration:** 12–18 months

## 4. Alternative Funders for the "Uncool" Operational Needs

Since CFLI will not fix SAJB's roof or pay the water bill, SAJB needs **parallel fundraising** for operational survival:

| Need | Potential Funder / Source |
|------|---------------------------|
| Building repair / plumbing | **Yayasan Hasanah** (Khazanah-backed; funds capital for registered NGOs)  <br> **EPF/CSR matching** via local developers (see existing "Developers with Active Foundations" list in this folder) |
| Rent subsidy / secure tenure | **Johor state welfare department (JKM)** — apply for leased premises or welfare facility designation  <br> **Local council (MBJB / MPJBT)** — community space rental waiver |
| Water bill relief | **SAJ (Syarikat Air Johor)** — community tariff discussion or leakage accountability claim against landlord  <br> **Zakat / wakaf funds** — temporary operational bridging |
| Gender-segregated space creation | **Corporate CSR** (furniture, partitions, modular fittings — easier to get in-kind than cash)  <br> **Religious institution donations** — mosque/temple fundraising for specific dignity upgrades |

## 5. Strategic Recommendation

**Do not** lead the CFLI application with SAJB's operational deficit. **Do** lead with the **rights-based problem** that SAJB's operational crisis illuminates.

**Recommended path:**
1. **Immediate:** Pursue Concept A (Gender-Responsive Transitional Housing Advocacy) for CFLI 2027 — it is the strongest alignment with SAJB's lived reality, has clear gender mainstreaming, and positions SAJB as a policy actor, not just a service delivery organisation.
2. **Parallel:** Use the "Developers with Active Foundations" list in this folder to approach 2–3 corporate CSR divisions for **in-kind building repair support** (plumbing, partitions, water tank).
3. **State level:** Draft a formal memorandum to the Johor State EXCO for Women, Family & Community Development requesting urgent intervention on gender-mixed shelter conditions — attach photos, water bills, and tenant testimony.

## 6. Red Flags to Avoid in Any CFLI Proposal

- ❌ "SAJB's halfway home is falling apart and we need money to fix it."
- ❌ "We house 30 men and women together and we need a new building."
- ❌ "Our water bill is too high."
- ✅ "Johor Bahru's transitional shelter ecosystem lacks gender-responsive standards, exposing women to dignity violations and GBV risks. SAJB, as a frontline community actor, proposes a participatory rights-based initiative to develop, pilot, and advocate for local adoption of safe shelter standards."
