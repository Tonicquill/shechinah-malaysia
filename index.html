Corporate CSR Programs and Transparent Social Impact Frameworks in Johor Bahru

Flagship Programme

Halfway House: Institutional Reintegration & Addiction Recovery Infrastructure

The Halfway House is the beating heart of Shechinah Malaysia. Located in Taman Rinting, Johor Bahru, it provides temporary shelter, daily meals, and structured rehabilitation for former inmates, recovering addicts, and families in crisis who have nowhere else to turn. Residents are not merely housed — they are walked through a reintegration pathway that includes job placement assistance, skills training, and counselling.

Many who arrive have been rejected by family, employers, and society. Shechinah operates on the belief that a prison sentence or addiction history is not a life sentence. The programme partners with local employers willing to give second chances, and maintains a network of counsellors who understand recovery firsthand. Every resident leaves with more than a bed — they leave with a plan.

Meals of Hope & Love: Localized B40 Food Security & Emergency Subsidized Relief

What began as an emergency COVID-19 food distribution in 2020 has become a permanent lifeline. 500+ meals are delivered monthly to the homeless, hungry, and homebound elderly across Johor Bahru — rain or shine, holiday or workday. The programme does not discriminate by race, religion, or background. If someone is hungry, they eat.

The Meals of Hope programme operates through a network of volunteer drivers, cooks, and coordinators who prepare and distribute hot meals, dry goods, and clean water to identified communities. Beyond food, the regular contact builds trust and relationships that open doors to deeper intervention — shelter referrals, medical connections, and family reunification. A meal is often the first handshake in a longer journey of restoration.

Community Development

Rural People Development: Orang Asli Community Infrastructure & Educational Upliftment

Shechinah serves 4 Orang Asli villages across Johor — not as outsiders bearing gifts, but as long-term partners. The Rural People Development programme delivers English tuition for children, clean water infrastructure, agricultural support, and job placement for rural youth seeking employment in nearby towns. The goal is not dependency. It is dignity through self-sufficiency.

Major interventions include flood relief to Kota Tinggi and Pasir Salam (January 2024), Wesak Day cultural engagement at Kampung Orang Asli Seletar, Sungai Temon (May 2024), and ongoing partnerships with Raffles University, Bayer Malaysia, and Newcastle Medical University for health screenings and educational support. When a village is strengthened, the whole region benefits.

Prayer & Counselling

Restoration is not only material. Shechinah's Prayer & Counselling programme provides emotional and spiritual support for domestic violence survivors, recovering addicts, and families in crisis. The counsellors are not detached professionals — many are volunteers who have walked through their own valleys and emerged with wisdom to share.

The programme operates from the Halfway House and through mobile outreach, meeting people where they are — under bridges, in hospital wards, and in living rooms where hope has run thin. Sessions are confidential, culturally sensitive, and free of charge. We do not proselytise. We listen, we pray for those who ask, and we walk alongside without judgment.

Vocational Empowerment: Economic Reintegration & Independent Financial Literacy Training

A bed and a meal buy time. A skill buys a future. Shechinah's Skills & Vocational Programmes provide job readiness training, vocational certification, and reintegration support for Halfway House residents and rural youth. Courses include basic computer literacy, construction skills, food handling certification, and interview coaching.

Graduates receive certificates recognised by local employers, and the programme maintains active job placement partnerships with businesses in Johor Bahru willing to hire former residents. The emphasis is on financial independence and long-term stability — not temporary relief. When a resident earns their first paycheque, the entire community is strengthened.

The Founder

10+ years of relentless compassion.

James Issachar serves as the active Chairman of Shechinah Malaysia and Director of Naja Safety Sdn Bhd, bridging corporate OSH compliance frameworks with community welfare relief.

James Issachar didn't set out to build a charity. He saw need on the streets of Johor Bahru — former inmates turned away by society, recovering addicts with nowhere to go, Orang Asli children without basic education — and he acted.

Since formally registering Shechinah in 2018 (PPM-006-01-19042018), James and his team of 50 volunteers have provided over 6,500 shelters, fed 3,000 families, and run programmes in 4 Orang Asli villages. In September 2025, he launched a RM30,000 fundraising campaign for a new outreach van — covered by The Star.

"We don't just give food or shelter. We walk alongside people until they can stand on their own."

Our Journey

Rehabilitation and Welfare Impact Statistics in Johor Bahru.
2018 — today.

April 2018
Shechinah Malaysia officially registered with the Registrar of Societies (PPM-006-01-19042018). The Halfway House opens in Taman Rinting, Johor Bahru.
2020 — 2021
COVID-19 hits. Shechinah pivots to food distribution, launching what becomes the permanent Meals of Hope & Love programme. Over 500 meals delivered monthly to this day.
January 2024
Major flood relief deployed to Kota Tinggi and Pasir Salam Orang Asli village. Emergency food, water, and temporary shelter provided.
May 2024
Wesak Day outreach to Kampung Orang Asli Seletar, Sungai Temon. Cultural engagement alongside material aid.
September 2025
First-ever fundraising luncheon held at Sangkar Krystal Convention Hall, Bandar Baru Uda. Over a decade of service culminates in a community-wide effort to fund a new outreach van. Covered by The Iskandarian.
December 2025
Christmas outreach: 500 meals and clothing packs distributed to the homeless in Johor Bahru. James: "We didn't stop in 2020, and we won't stop now."
Today — 2026
50 volunteers. 5 active programmes. 4 Orang Asli villages. New partnerships with Raffles University, Bayer Malaysia, and Newcastle Medical University. And we're just getting started.
